Survival Curves by Dosage

A simulated 365-day trial of 250 patients in three arms. Filter the patients and compare arms: Kaplan-Meier curves show the share still alive each day, the log-rank test asks whether two curves differ, and the chi-square test asks whether adverse-event rates differ.

  1. Choose patients by gender and age, or switch to Your own data and paste any time-to-event data.
  2. Tick the arms to compare.
  3. Read the curves, then the tests below them. A dropping line means patients dying; the dashed line marks half still alive.
Data

One row per subject: group, time, event. Event is 1 if it happened (death, relapse, a customer cancelling) and 0 if the subject was still fine when follow-up ended (censored). Commas, semicolons or tabs all work, so a spreadsheet column copy pastes straight in. The example is a real, published trial: Freireich and colleagues (1963), weeks of leukemia remission on 6-MP against placebo, where the textbook answer is a log-rank statistic of 16.79. Nothing leaves your browser.

Arms

From Clinical Trials Patient Outcomes Analysis (FINDINGS). The patients are simulated with a fixed seed; none are real. An adverse event is the project's own rule: systolic blood pressure under 105 or over 155 at any visit. Every deploy checks the page against lifelines and SciPy on all 12 gender and age filters, and on the own-data example.
