Survival Curves by Dosage

A simulated 365-day trial of 250 patients in three arms. Filter the patients and compare arms: Kaplan-Meier curves show the share still alive each day, the log-rank test asks whether two curves differ, and the chi-square test asks whether adverse-event rates differ.

  1. Choose patients by gender and age, or switch to Your own data and paste any time-to-event data.
  2. Tick the arms to compare.
  3. Read the curves, then the tests below them. A dropping line means patients dying; the dashed line marks half still alive.
Data

One row per subject: group, time, event. Event is 1 if it happened (death, relapse, a customer cancelling) and 0 if the subject was still fine when follow-up ended (censored). Commas, semicolons or tabs all work, so a spreadsheet column copy pastes straight in. The example is a real, published trial: Freireich and colleagues (1963), weeks of leukemia remission on 6-MP against placebo, where the textbook answer is a log-rank statistic of 16.79.

Benford's Law Audit

In many real ledgers about 30% of amounts start with a 1 and under 5% with a 9 (Benford's Law). Made-up amounts often break that pattern. Test a synthetic ledger of 1,200 tax claims with 47 planted anomalies, or paste your own amounts; then tune the risk score that ranks entries for review.

  1. Pick a slice of the ledger, or paste amounts.
  2. Compare the bars (your amounts) with the dots (Benford's Law), and read the fit test.
  3. Move the sliders to reweight the risk score and see how many planted anomalies it catches.

Every deploy checks the page against the project's Python, including the 30/70 weights and the 43 entries FINDINGS reports.

Fraud Rule Tester

Three simple rules screen 345,524 simulated card transactions, 2,591 of them planted fraud. Loosen or tighten each rule and see the trade-off a fraud team lives with: catching more fraud means more false alarms on real customers.

  1. Load the simulated transactions (about 3.8 MB, once), or switch to Your own transactions and paste or upload a CSV.
  2. Move the sliders: how many charges in how many minutes count as card testing, and how large a purchase counts as a spike.
  3. Watch precision (share of alerts that are fraud) and recall (share of fraud caught) move.
Data

One row per charge: customer, time, amount, then optionally foreign (1 or 0, or a location; blank, 0, no or domestic count as home) and fraud (1 or 0, only to score the rules). Time is a date and time (2026-03-01 14:05) or a number of seconds. Without a fraud column you see the alerts but not precision or recall. The example below is made up to show each rule once; replace it with an export from a bank or card statement. It is read in your browser and never uploaded.

From FinTech Credit Risk & Fraud Command Center (FINDINGS). The transactions are simulated with a fixed seed and the fraud is planted by the simulator; the rules never read the labels. The rules are etl/fraud_rules.py rewritten for the browser; every deploy checks them against the Python at five settings, and through the CSV upload path, and they must agree on every count.

LinkedIn Post Checker

CurioSync drafts LinkedIn posts from project notes and will not queue one until it passes these writing checks: length, a short first line, no stock AI phrases ("delve", "game-changer", "not just X but Y"), no engagement bait, no word or number repeated too often, and paragraphs that each move the story on.

  1. Paste your own draft, or pick a sample: a post CurioSync queued and test drafts written to break the rules.
  2. Edit the text; the checks rerun as you type.
  3. Read what CurioSync would send back for a rewrite.

From CurioSync: writing_problems in app/services/post_rules.py, rewritten for the browser. Its fact checks (a number or tool the project's notes do not back, "we" on solo work) need the project's own facts, so they do not run here; a draft that passes on this page can still be held back by them. Every deploy checks this page against the Python on 15 published and test drafts: the same problems, word for word, in the same order.
