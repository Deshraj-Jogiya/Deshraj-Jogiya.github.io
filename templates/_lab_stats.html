Survival Curves by Dosage

A simulated 365-day trial of 250 patients in three arms. Filter the patients and compare arms: Kaplan-Meier curves show the share still alive each day, the log-rank test asks whether two curves differ, and the chi-square test asks whether adverse-event rates differ.

  1. Choose patients by gender and age, or switch to Your own data and paste any time-to-event data.
  2. Tick the arms to compare.
  3. Read the curves, then the tests below them. A dropping line means patients dying; the dashed line marks half still alive.
Data

One row per subject: group, time, event. Event is 1 if it happened (death, relapse, a customer cancelling) and 0 if the subject was still fine when follow-up ended (censored). Commas, semicolons or tabs all work, so a spreadsheet column copy pastes straight in. The example is a real, published trial: Freireich and colleagues (1963), weeks of leukemia remission on 6-MP against placebo, where the textbook answer is a log-rank statistic of 16.79.

Benford's Law Audit

In many real ledgers about 30% of amounts start with a 1 and under 5% with a 9 (Benford's Law). Made-up amounts often break that pattern. Test a synthetic ledger of 1,200 tax claims with 47 planted anomalies, or paste your own amounts; then tune the risk score that ranks entries for review.

  1. Pick a slice of the ledger, or paste amounts.
  2. Compare the bars (your amounts) with the dots (Benford's Law), and read the fit test.
  3. Move the sliders to reweight the risk score and see how many planted anomalies it catches.

Fraud Rule Tester

Three simple rules screen 345,524 simulated card transactions, 2,591 of them planted fraud. Loosen or tighten each rule and see the trade-off a fraud team lives with: catching more fraud means more false alarms on real customers.

  1. Load the transactions (about 3.8 MB, once).
  2. Move the sliders: how many charges in how many minutes count as card testing, and how large a purchase counts as a spike.
  3. Watch precision (share of alerts that are fraud) and recall (share of fraud caught) move.

From FinTech Credit Risk & Fraud Command Center (FINDINGS). The transactions are simulated with a fixed seed and the fraud is planted by the simulator; the rules never read the labels. The rules are etl/fraud_rules.py rewritten for the browser; every deploy checks them against the Python at five settings, and they must agree on every count.
